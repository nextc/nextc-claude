# Lens: Jobs-to-be-Done

Sources: Christensen ("milkshake"), Ulwick (Outcome-Driven Innovation), Moesta and Spiek (Forces of Progress).

**Fits when** the question is whether people will switch to something new, or what they are really
trying to get done. **Skip when** the question is about defensibility, build-vs-buy, or market
structure, and the job is already obvious from the evidence.

## Method

1. Write the core functional job: "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome]."
   It must still exist if every current product disappeared. Ground the situation in evidence
   records (reviews, forum posts, founder statements) and cite them.
2. Name the emotional and social jobs, but only those the evidence shows. Unsupported ones are
   judgment; mark them so.
3. List what people hire today for this job, including non-products (a spreadsheet, a friend,
   doing nothing). Use the prior-art records; add any the evidence suggests.
4. Forces of progress: push (pain now), pull (appeal of new), anxiety (fear of switching), habit.
   Switching happens only when push + pull > anxiety + habit. Say which force is weakest in the
   evidence.

## Anti-patterns

- The job is a feature ("use our app") or too broad to act on ("be happy").
- Emotional jobs invented to make the idea sound bigger.
- Treating a persona as a job.

## Lens section of the output

Job statement; what is hired today (with claim ids); forces table (force | evidence | strength);
the force that decides switching, and how to measure it.
