# Lens: Assumption Reversal

**Fits when** the category has strong conventions and the founder's idea follows them. **Skip
when** the evidence pack shows the category is new or has no settled conventions.

## Method

1. Name the category and its main players (from prior-art records).
2. List five load-bearing assumptions every player follows (pricing model, who pays, where it is
   used, ownership, onboarding, distribution). "If every competitor does X, X is an assumption."
3. Invert each one structurally, not cosmetically, and state the candidate idea it produces.
4. For each inversion: what has changed (technology, regulation, behaviour) that makes it feasible
   now, with evidence where it exists; and what would have to be true for it to work.
5. Rank the two most promising inversions.

## Anti-patterns

- Reversing facts of nature rather than industry conventions.
- Cosmetic inversions (colour, naming).
- Treating "hard to imagine" as evidence against an inversion.

## Lens section of the output

Assumption | held by | inversion | candidate idea | why now (claim id or judgment); the top two
with what would have to be true.
