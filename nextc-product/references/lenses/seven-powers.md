# Lens: 7 Powers

Source: Hamilton Helmer. A power needs both a benefit and a barrier that stops rivals from
copying the benefit.

**Fits when** the question is whether a business can last or charge more over time. **Skip when**
the founder's success criterion is not commercial (for example, a free tool for a community) and
durability is not in question.

## Method

1. Take the competitive set from prior-art records.
2. For each power (scale economies, network economies, counter-positioning, switching costs,
   branding, cornered resource, process power) say yes / no / conditional in one line each.
3. For the one or two plausible powers: the benefit, the barrier, what would have to be true, and
   what would kill it.
4. Say honestly when no power is plausible. Most early products have none.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating better UX, a better team or being first as a power.
- Calling a weak local network a network effect.
- Switching costs without naming what the customer would have to redo.

## Lens section of the output

Seven one-line verdicts; the plausible power(s) with benefit, barrier, conditions and kill
switch; whether durability matters to the founder's stated goal.
