# Strategic Product Naming for ASO

> **Status of this playbook (header added 2026-11-16).** These are practitioner heuristics, dated as
> written; most claims (lift percentages, algorithm behaviour, "2025–2026 shifts", the "July 2025" date)
> carry no source and were not re-verified. Treat them as hypotheses to test with observed data.
> Store limits and policy rules come from `scripts/limits.json`, which names the official source and
> the date each value was checked; where this file and `limits.json` disagree, `limits.json` wins.
> Limits corrected on 2026-11-16: Google Play title is 30 characters (not 50); the iOS keyword field
> is 100 bytes (not characters).

A reusable framework for naming apps that are **brandable**, **searchable**, and **safe** — and that maximize organic install ceilings from day one.

---

## Why Naming Is the Highest-Leverage ASO Decision

Naming gets priced once and pays out for the life of the product. The title is the single strongest ranking signal on Google Play and a top-3 signal on iOS — both stores use your app name for keyword indexation. Renaming later is technically possible but costs you accumulated ranking authority and word-of-mouth memory.

Two compounding effects most teams miss:

- **Brand awareness flows back through brand-term searches.** Every podcast mention, every social post, every press hit becomes a search query. If your name is hard to spell or collides with a bigger app, that traffic leaks to the wrong destination.
- **The title is the only field that gets ranking weight from both stores at top priority.** Subtitles and descriptions matter, but nothing else indexes with the same authority.

Get this right at launch.

---

## 1. Core Formula

```text
Title    = Brand + strongest keyword angle
Subtitle = keyword expansion covering intent NOT already in title
```

The brand makes the product ownable. The keywords make it discoverable. A brand-only title wastes your most powerful ranking real estate.

Bad:    `Lunavo`
Better: `Lunavo: Sleep Stories`
Strongest: `Lunavo: Sleep Better`

---

## 2. Character Economics

You get 30 characters for the title on both iOS and Google Play (`limits.json`, checked 2026-11-16). Give the brand portion 5–15 characters and leave the rest for one or two generic keywords.

Long invented brand names burn the entire budget on something only converted users will search for, while leaving zero room for the keywords new users actually type.

```text
Brand portion:   5–15 chars
Separator:       : or — or ·
Keyword portion: 12–22 chars
```

---

## 3. Stage-Based Strategy

Brand-term search volume depends on whether anyone knows you yet. Optimize for the stage your product is actually in.

**New apps (no brand recognition)**
- Lead with generic, descriptive keywords
- Brand is decorative until you build awareness
- Example: `Habitly: Habit Tracker`

**Established apps (known brand)**
- Brand becomes the primary search term
- Generic keywords become secondary
- Example: `Calm: Sleep & Meditation`

Most products live in the new-app stage for the first 6–18 months. Optimize for that period, not the post-PMF dream state. You can rename the keyword portion later for free; you can't reclaim 18 months of buried rankings.

## 4. The Four Keyword Angles

Every title should pick one of these four angles. For each new product, draft titles in all four directions and choose based on keyword research, not gut.

### A. Category — what the app is

Use when users already search the category directly. Default choice when clarity matters more than cleverness.

| Product | Title | Subtitle |
|---|---|---|
| Habit app | `HabitDay: Daily Habits` | `Small challenges for self growth` |
| Baby diary | `BloomNote: Baby Journal` | `Firsts, memories, milestones` |
| Mood app | `InnerMirror: Mood Journal` | `Reflect on feelings and patterns` |
| Habit app | `TinyWins: Habit Tracker` | `Build routines without pressure` |

### B. Result — what the user gets

Use when the outcome is more searched, more emotional, or more attractive than the tool category. Higher click-through but smaller pool.

| Product | Title | Subtitle |
|---|---|---|
| Sleep app | `TinyCalm: Sleep Better` | `Sounds, stories, meditation` |
| Focus app | `FocusFlow: Study Better` | `Timer, tasks, and deep work` |
| Budget app | `ClearSpend: Save Money` | `Budget planner and expense tracker` |
| AI coach | `NextStep: Get Unstuck` | `AI coach for goals and decisions` |

### C. Niche — specific audience or use case

Use when broad keywords are unwinnable but a sub-segment has clearer intent. Best for finding an organic beachhead in a saturated category.

| Product | Title | Subtitle |
|---|---|---|
| Pregnancy app | `FirstBloom: Pregnancy Journal` | `Baby memories and milestones` |
| Stoic app | `Aurea: Stoic Journal` | `Daily reflection and wisdom prompts` |
| ADHD app | `ClearStep: ADHD Planner` | `Simple tasks, focus, routines` |
| Family app | `KinCircle: Family Calendar` | `Shared plans, reminders, chores` |
| Expat app | `Trace: Expat Friend Map` | `Meet trusted people nearby` |

### D. Anti-category — what the app rejects

Use when the market is crowded and users actively dislike the incumbents. Differentiation is more important than category conformity.

| Product | Title | Subtitle |
|---|---|---|
| Habit app | `NoStreak: Gentle Habits` | `Routines without pressure` |
| Journal app | `QuietNote: Private Journal` | `No feeds, likes, or noise` |
| Planner | `SlowPlan: Calm Planner` | `Tasks, goals, and daily focus` |
| Social app | `CloseCircle: Private Social` | `Friends, family, trusted groups` |
| To-do app | `SoftTasks: To Do List` | `Simple planning without overwhelm` |

## 5. Brand Name Strategy

The brand portion can do double duty as a keyword if you derive it from a high-value generic term. This is the smartest ASO naming move available.

| Generic Keyword | Brand Variants |
|---|---|
| habit | `Habitly`, `Habito`, `TinyHabit`, `HabitLoop` |
| sleep | `Sleepora`, `Sleeply`, `Dreamly`, `Restia` |
| habit | `Habitly`, `HabitDay`, `Habitora`, `HabitPath` |
| note | `Notely`, `NoteNest`, `MemoNote`, `NoteFlow` |
| mood | `Moodly`, `MoodMirror`, `MoodNest` |
| task | `Tasky`, `Tasklet`, `SoftTasks` |
| budget | `Budgetly`, `Spendly`, `ClearSpend` |
| photo | `Photoly`, `SnapSort`, `CleanPix` |
| calm | `Calma`, `TinyCalm`, `DailyCalm` |
| focus | `Focusly`, `FocusFlow`, `Focusa` |

The strongest version is the keyword compound (TinyCalm, ClearSpend, BloomNote) — clearer than typo-style names, easier to pronounce, more brandable.

---

## 6. Brand Variation Types

### A. Keyword compound (strongest)

Two real words joined cleanly. Pronounceable, brandable, type-safely.

Examples: `HabitDay`, `BloomNote`, `TinyCalm`, `ClearSpend`, `SoftTasks`, `FocusFlow`

### B. Phonetic brand

A word that sounds close to a useful keyword but feels like its own brand.

Examples: `Calma`, `Focusa`, `Spendly`, `Tasky`, `Moodly`, `Routinely`, `Dreamly`, `Notely`

Good for consumer apps where the name should feel friendly and memorable.

### C. Natural spelling twist

Drop a vowel, add an "ly", soften a letter. Must still pronounce cleanly.

Examples: `Memorii`, `Plannr`, `Storii`, `Journly`, `Focuz`, `Lingua`, `Ritualy`

**Caveat:** every dropped vowel makes word-of-mouth leak. Test the name out loud before committing. If a friend tells someone "you should try Memorii," what do they type into search?

## 7. Safe vs Risky

| Type | Example | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Keyword compound | `HabitDay`, `TinyCalm`, `ClearSpend` | **Best** |
| Variation of generic keyword | `Routinely`, `Habitly`, `Sleepora` | Good |
| Phonetic brand from keyword | `Ritualy`, `Focusa`, `Spendly` | Good |
| Natural typo of generic keyword | `Memorii`, `Plannr`, `Calma` | OK with caveats |
| Typo of competitor app | `Notionn`, `Calmm`, `Duolingoo`, `Tindr` | **Never** |
| Slight variation of famous brand | `Canvaa`, `Bumbel`, `Stravaa` | **Never** |
| Competitor name in title/subtitle | `Better than Notion`, `Tinder Alternative` | **Never** |

### Why the "never" line matters

Typo or lookalike versions of famous apps violate both stores' impersonation policies and trigger trademark disputes. Apple's Guideline 4.1 specifically prohibits minor variations of another app's name or UI. Consequences range from forced rename to developer account termination.

The downside is asymmetric — small upside, existential downside. Never worth it for a serious business.

### The rule

```text
Safe:  variation of a generic high-ranked keyword
Risky: variation of a famous app, company, or trademark
```

---

## 8. Two Hard Rules Often Missed

### Rule A — Type-safe for word-of-mouth

If someone hears your name in a podcast or voice memo, can they type it into search without guessing? Names with dropped vowels (Plannr, Memorii) leak referral traffic. Names that share spelling with their generic keyword origin are safer.

Test: say the name to three people, ask them to spell it. If they hesitate, redesign.

### Rule B — Free of name collision

Before committing, search the name in both stores. If a popular app already owns the brand-term search results page, you'll never rank for your own name.

This is the silent killer of new launches — you compete for your own brand traffic before you ever compete for category traffic. A name that's "creative" but collides with an existing top-10 app in any major category is a poisoned well.

## 9. Subtitle Strategy

The subtitle is a separate keyword field. Do NOT repeat words from the title — both stores ignore duplicates and you waste characters.

```text
Title    = Brand + strongest keyword angle
Subtitle = 2–4 supporting keywords covering different intent
```

**Bad (repeats):**
```
Title:    BloomNote: Baby Journal
Subtitle: A journal for your baby     ← reuses "journal" and "baby"
```

**Better (expands):**
```
Title:    BloomNote: Baby Journal
Subtitle: Firsts, memories, milestones
```

**Bad (repeats):**
```
Title:    NoStreak: Gentle Habits
Subtitle: Gentle habit tracker        ← reuses "gentle" and "habit"
```

**Better (expands):**
```
Title:    NoStreak: Gentle Habits
Subtitle: Routines without pressure
```

## 10. Platform Differences That Affect Naming

| Aspect | iOS | Google Play |
|---|---|---|
| Title char limit | 30 | 30 |
| Title weight | High | Highest |
| Subtitle | Yes (30 chars, indexed) | No (use short description, 80 chars) |
| Keyword field | Hidden, 100 bytes | None |
| Spell correction | Imperfect — typo keywords work | Stronger — typo keywords less useful |
| Year/version in title | Discouraged | Against guidelines |

Google Play gives you more room in the title and weights it heavier. The Brand + Keyword formula works on both, but on Google Play you can sometimes extend further if it reads naturally:

```text
iOS:         BloomNote: Baby Journal
Google Play: BloomNote: Baby Journal - Memories & Milestones
```

## 13. Final Rules

1. **Always include both brand and keyword.** Brand-only titles waste your strongest field.
2. **The brand can be a natural variation of a generic high-value keyword.** Habitly, Notely, Sleepora.
3. **Never use typo variants of famous apps or trademarks.** The downside is existential.
4. **The title owns one keyword angle.** The subtitle expands with non-overlapping keywords.
5. **Test all four naming directions before deciding.** Category, Result, Niche, Anti-category.
6. **Optimize for the new-app stage.** Most products live there for 6–18 months.
7. **Run the collision check before committing.** Both stores + Google + trademark database.
8. **Run the word-of-mouth test.** If three people can't spell it, redesign.
